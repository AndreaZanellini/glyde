What this mockup asks you to decide

It draws the real 01_engine_testbench.csv showcase file. Try it in both themes, at 1440 × 900, and with the 20-state-channel stress test. Merging the PR means you approve it; the egui app is then changed to match, one roadmap item at a time.

Layout (#230, #200, #198)

  1. One time axis, at the bottom only. Numeric and state tracks share it. The date and "local time, no zone" sit at its left, so the inferred time zone is always visible.
  2. Stacked is the default for numeric channels: one plot per channel, each with its own y range that follows the visible data. Overlaid puts them on one plot with one y axis.
  3. At most 8 numeric channels on screen, one per palette color. On open, the first 8 in file order are shown; the rest are hidden and the inference bar says how many. Enabling a ninth asks you to hide one first.
  4. State channels take at most 32 % of the time view. Beyond that the group scrolls inside itself; it can be collapsed to one row. Text states are 22 px bands, booleans 12 px strips, markers ticks with a label when there is room.
  5. Gaps are hatched across every track, with their length on the axis.
  6. The spectrum lives in a bottom split, 40 % of the height by default and resizable. Its controls do not move, its legend sits outside the plot.

Reading values (#196, #197, #231)

  1. Hover: where one sample covers the pointer, its exact value and timestamp. Where a pixel holds many samples, the min–max range of those samples and how many there are, never one arbitrary sample.
  2. Zoom acts on time only. Every y range follows the visible data. You cannot zoom out past the file.
  3. Cursors A and B run across every track. The panel on the right shows Δt, 1/Δt and, per channel, the value at A and B, ΔY and ΔY/Δt. Each cursor snaps to the nearest sample and shows that sample's timestamp.

Look (#229)

  1. Palette: cool graphite neutrals, a petrol accent used only for UI state, and an 8-color data palette checked for color-blind separation in both themes. Text states use soft tints with ink labels so they never look like a numeric series.
  2. Type: Inter for the interface, JetBrains Mono for timestamps and values so digits line up. Both are SIL Open Font License; embedding them should add under 1 MB to the binary.
  3. Light and dark follow the OS. The switch in the mockup bar is only here so you can compare; the app has no theme setting.

Not in this mockup

  • Horizontal cursors for a pure ΔY (optional in SPEC §4.1): proposed as a later step on the same panel.
  • The compressed (gap) axis: this file's gaps are 20 % of its span, so Glyde correctly does not offer it.
  • The app icon (#232). The mark in the toolbar is a placeholder.

Mockup limits

To stay one page, it carries a 250 ms min/max overview of the whole file and every raw sample in three windows: cranking, the 09:03:00 throttle release, and the misfire. Outside those windows the cursors say the sample is not in the mockup; the app reads it from the file. The two spectra were computed ahead of time with Glyde's own default settings.
